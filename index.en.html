How I learn English day to dayMy routine · English definitions, repeated exposure, daily reading, and pronunciation basicsRead experience ＋Close experience −

Personal experience and opinions, not sponsored. Hover to expand, or click the heading or use Enter / Space. Press Esc to collapse.

  1. 01 / Explain English in English

    I read English definitions. When an unfamiliar synonym appears, I open its entry too, following the connections between words to understand them.

  2. 02 / Review daily, without expecting instant mastery

    My daily goal is to go through the material once. Repeated exposure over time lets words reappear in different contexts, rather than expecting myself to remember them in one sitting.

  3. 03 / Read daily and try thinking in English

    I read with Shanbay every day and try to think directly in English as I read. This is a personal habit, not a sponsored recommendation.

  4. 04 / Start pronunciation with the basics

    I think pronunciation can be built gradually from the basics. Shanbay Speaking or similar tools can support practice; choose what suits you rather than treating one tool as the only route.

What others do

Meet vocabulary again through listening, reading, and use. Actively recall new words, notice them in context, and record and practice examples connected to everyday life.

Scope:British Council teaching advice, not an effectiveness trial of a daily streak plan or a guarantee of how long progress will take.

Preparing short talks and getting unstuck in writingLearning skills · Short talks, data provenance, and finding a way through writing blocksRead experience ＋Close experience −

A summary of the author’s personal experience, not a verbatim quotation. Hover to expand, or click the heading or use Enter / Space. Press Esc to collapse.

  1. 01 / Help a new audience understand the background

    For a short talk, I start with people hearing about the project for the first time: explain the background, then the results, implications, and future directions. Clearly credit data from other lab members.

  2. 02 / Revisit the project’s value when writing feels stuck

    When writing stalls, it can help to revisit why the project matters and which questions still deserve discussion. Notice whether self-critical “encouragement” is making the task harder instead of simply pushing yourself more.
